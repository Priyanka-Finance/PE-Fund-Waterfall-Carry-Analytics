## How the waterfall distributed cash

LPs paid in $466.1m over the life of the fund. Tier 1 returned that capital in full ($466.1m). Tier 2 paid $144.1m of preferred return: the 8% hurdle, compounded annually, on capital for as long as it stayed outstanding. Tier 3 gave the GP a catch-up of $36.0m; it takes every dollar after the hurdle until its carry equals 20% of all profit distributed. Tier 4 split the rest 80/20: $243.5m to LPs and $60.9m to the GP. Carried interest distributed therefore totals $96.9m, 20.0% of LP-class profit, which is exactly what a completed catch-up is designed to deliver.

Under whole-of-fund terms nothing reaches the GP until LPs have received all contributed capital plus the preferred return on it. That is why the first carry was paid only in December 2029, 7.8 years into the fund's life, even though distributions began earlier. In that quarter the LP class had $93.3m to distribute: $1.7m returned capital, $8.7m paid preferred return, $36.0m went to the GP as catch-up and the remainder was split $37.4m to LPs and $9.4m to the GP.

## Whole-of-fund versus deal-by-deal

Run on identical deals, the two waterfalls pay the GP the same carry in total ($96.9m) but on very different dates. First carry arrives in March 2026 under deal-by-deal against December 2029 under whole-of-fund, and the average dollar of carry arrives 1.7 years earlier. Earlier carry for the GP is later cash for LPs: deal-by-deal moves LP net IRR from 15.2% to 14.5%, a cost of 68 bps, while the present value of the GP's carry at a 10% discount rate rises by $8.3m. The risk LPs take in exchange is over-distribution, although on the base case the GP was never ahead of its whole-of-fund entitlement. ## Risks and watch-points

- **Concentration.** Three deals produce 45.1% of the gain and Project Juno alone 17.0%; a miss on any of them moves the fund.
- **Key assumption.** LP returns are most sensitive to exit multiples: a swing of 866 bps in net IRR across the range tested.
- **Fee drag.** Management fees cost 307 bps of IRR, more than carried interest (218 bps), and are paid whatever the outcome; they total 11.7% of commitments.
- **Clawback under deal-by-deal terms.** A material clawback arises in 8.6% of simulated outcomes and the after-tax limit means LPs would not recover all of it. No carry is held in escrow to secure it.
- **Unrealised value.** At September 2026 RVPI of 1.14x exceeds DPI of 0.31x: most of the reported 1.45x TVPI rests on marks, which in this model follow a stated valuation policy rather than market evidence.
- **Hurdle risk.** LPs fall short of the 8% preferred return in 7.1% of simulated outcomes.
